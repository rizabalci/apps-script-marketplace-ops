How it fits together

Data comes in through official APIs, lands in Google Sheets, and turns into dashboards and actions. A person always makes the call.

Four rules behind every tool

Operations tooling fails quietly. These rules are there so it never does.

Read-only on marketplaces

Collectors only call GET endpoints. Nothing touches listings, orders, prices or settings.

Drafts, never sends

Partner emails are Gmail drafts with the evidence attached. A human reviews and hits send.

Never invent a number

Missing data shows as blank or stale. Nothing is estimated to make a chart look complete.

Fail loud, once

Every job is guarded and heartbeat-checked. One email a day per problem, not a flood.

01Apps Script

Marketplace Health Collector

Account health from three marketplaces in one sheet, filled before anyone starts work.

The problem
Account health lived in three seller portals with different metrics, windows and logins. Checking them by hand every morning was slow, easy to skip, and a bad week was only noticed once the account was already at risk.
What I built
  • Scheduled collectors for the Amazon SP-API seller performance report (all EU marketplaces), bol performance indicators and Otto orders.
  • A weekly late-dispatch-by-warehouse table pulled straight from the ERP database over read-only SQL.
  • A heartbeat tab: every job stamps itself, and a daily check emails when one stops running.
  • A ManoMano monitor for delivery, tracking, cancellation and return deadlines.
Result
The morning portal round is gone. Health data arrives on its own, and a silent failure becomes an email instead of a gap nobody sees.

02Apps Script

Prime Badge Check Dashboard

Is every product Prime in every region today, and is the delivery promise slipping?

The problem
Losing the Prime badge in some postcodes costs sales quietly. Nobody notices until conversion drops, and the checks that did exist were spread over a spreadsheet nobody could read at a glance.
What I built
  • A web dashboard reading check blocks (product by postcode: badge yes or no, promised delivery day) and showing trends across checks.
  • Product groups by size class, so an oversize problem stands out from a single bad SKU.
  • A watchdog that emails when an expected check block is missing, plus EU account health views and a US alert email.
  • The shared action engine, turning a lost badge into an owned task.
Result
One page answers the Prime question in seconds. Incomplete checks never count as fresh data, so a quiet collector can't hide a problem.

03Apps Script

Account Health Dashboard

A red metric, and the date it clears on its own.

The problem
Amazon's late dispatch, valid tracking and on-time delivery rates run on rolling windows. When one turns red, the real question is whether to act now or whether the bad orders roll out next week. Nobody could answer that quickly.
What I built
  • A single-marketplace health page showing each metric against Amazon's target.
  • Window maths that works out when today's bad orders leave each 10-day and 30-day window.
  • An optional ingest endpoint for an importer to add readings, locked and de-duplicated.
  • Rules that turn a breach into an owner, a deadline and a drafted email.
Result
"Do we need to do something?" gets a dated answer instead of a guess, and real breaches go straight into an owned action.

04Apps Script

SFP Weekly Dashboard

Seller Fulfilled Prime performance and headroom, counted in orders.

The problem
SFP is judged weekly on strict thresholds, and percentages hide the real margin. "93.9%" sounds safe until you see it means two more late orders and the programme is at risk.
What I built
  • A weekly dashboard for on-time delivery, tracking, cancellations and per-tier performance.
  • Headroom shown in orders, not just percent, for every size tier.
  • A freshness strip: amber at 8 days since the last read, red at 10.
  • Partner notes become a drafted email to the right warehouse.
Result
The weekly SFP review starts from "how many orders can we still miss" instead of a spreadsheet hunt.

05Apps Script

Ops Action Engine

From "the dashboard is red" to "who does what by when".

The problem
Dashboards show problems, but problems don't fix themselves. Escalations to warehouses were written from scratch, sent late, or repeated every day for the same issue.
What I built
  • A small reusable engine: rules return findings with severity, owner, evidence and next step.
  • An action panel for any dashboard, visible only to the owner by default.
  • Partner emails as Gmail drafts with an .xlsx of only the affected rows.
  • Fingerprinting, so a draft is only redone when the data changes or a week has passed.
  • A daily alert email and an open-actions log with age per item.
Result
Every red finding has an owner and a ready-to-send draft. No duplicate nagging, nothing sent without a human.

06Looker Studio

Marketplace Account Health Report

One page per marketplace: latest reading, headroom and history.

The problem
Management wanted one shareable place to see account health across Amazon EU, Otto and bol, without opening Apps Script pages or raw sheets.
What I built
  • A Looker Studio report with a page per marketplace, built on the collector's sheet.
  • Scorecards for the latest reading and headroom in units, plus trend charts.

07Looker Studio

SFP US Control Room

US Seller Fulfilled Prime: on-time per size tier and why packages were late.

The problem
A US SFP trial needed daily visibility across several US warehouses and time zones. Late packages had to be split by cause, since a carrier delay and a missed pick need different fixes.
What I built
  • A summary banner and an on-time gauge per size tier.
  • Late packages per judged week, split by cause.
  • A late-package table whose timestamps keep their time-zone offset, so the cause is never misread.
Result
The daily catch-up starts from the same screen for everyone, and causes are settled with timestamps, not opinions.

08Looker Studio

3PL Warehouse Scorecard

A weekly scorecard warehouses can't argue with.

The problem
Partner reviews were based on feelings and single bad orders. Without a shared weekly view, every escalation turned into a debate about whose fault it was.
What I built
  • An 8-page weekly scorecard per warehouse site: overview heatmaps and week over week.
  • Same-day dispatch, partner acceptance KPIs and dispatch buckets.
  • Late notes by reason, and release to label to carrier-scan timing.
  • Cost per parcel by site.
Result
Reviews start from the same numbers on both sides. What the warehouse controls is kept apart from what the carrier controls, so the data holds up. Sites are scored, never individual people.

09Looker Studio

Landed Cost Monitor

Per-unit cost by marketplace and channel, with the gaps in plain sight.

The problem
Margin looked fine on average while some SKUs and channels lost money. Surcharge percentages were compared across carriers on different base rates, which says nothing about the real cost.
What I built
  • Per-unit COGS, shipping and fees by marketplace and channel from a profit export.
  • A shipping outlier view, and a list of SKUs with no COGS at all.
  • A separate US page by marketplace and fulfilment channel.
  • Plain labelling of what the export covers, so it is never sold as full landed cost when postage is missing.
Result
Cost talks compare per-unit numbers, not percentages, and missing data shows up as a to-do instead of a silent zero.

10Toolkit

Marketplace Ops Toolkit

The nine tools as one system, and this page.

The problem
Single dashboards help one person. An operation needs collection, visibility and follow-up to work together, with the same rules everywhere, or the analysis happens once and then stops.
What I built
  • An index repo linking every template, with shared principles and setup notes.
  • One config pattern across projects: IDs and addresses in one file, secrets in Script Properties.
  • The same guard, heartbeat and drafts-only rules in every tool.
Result
Recurring checks run on their own. The work moves from collecting numbers to deciding what to do about them.
